The bar chart delineates the distribution of average household expenditure across six principal categories in Canada for the year 2024.

In summary, housing constituted the most substantial financial outlay, absorbing nearly a third of the total budget, while the remaining categories accounted for comparatively modest and relatively balanced proportions. A clear hierarchy of spending is evident, with essential shelter costs dominating all other expenditures.

Specifically, housing commanded 32% of the average household budget, a figure that is more than double the allocation for any other single category. Transport represented the next highest expense at 17%, followed by utilities and leisure, which constituted 12% and 11% respectively. Food and the miscellaneous ‘other’ category each accounted for an equal 8% of total spending, representing the smallest shares.

Referencing the detailed breakdown, the dominance of housing at 32% starkly contrasts with the minimal 8% apportioned to food and other items, indicating the significant financial pressure exerted by accommodation costs. The 17% spent on transport is notably higher than the 12% for utilities, suggesting that mobility and home energy are secondary but distinct priorities. Furthermore, the parity between leisure (11%) and utilities (12%) reveals a comparable investment in recreation and essential household operations.

In conclusion, the data underscores a pronounced imbalance in Canadian household spending, with shelter costs overwhelming other areas of consumption and leaving a tightly constrained portion of the budget for all remaining needs.
