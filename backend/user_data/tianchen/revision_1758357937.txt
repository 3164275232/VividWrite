The chart illustrates the volume of telephone calls in Australia across three categories—local fixed line calls, national and international fixed line calls, and mobile calls—from 2001 to 2008. 

Overall, it is evident that while local fixed line calls experienced a decline over the period, both national and international fixed line calls and mobile calls saw a significant increase. 

In detail, local fixed line calls started at 73 billion minutes in 2001, peaking at 90 billion minutes in 2005 before declining to 73 billion minutes by 2008. Conversely, national and international fixed line calls increased steadily from 38 billion minutes in 2001 to 58 billion minutes in 2008, with a notable rise in the latter years. Mobile calls exhibited the most pronounced growth, escalating from merely 3 billion minutes in 2001 to 48 billion minutes by 2008, indicating a shift in communication preferences among Australians. 

The data reflects a clear transition from traditional fixed line communication methods towards mobile technology over the seven-year period.
