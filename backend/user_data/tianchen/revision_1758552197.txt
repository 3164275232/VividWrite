The chart illustrates the trends in telephone call categories in Australia from 2001 to 2008, encompassing local fixed line calls, national and international fixed line calls, and mobile calls.

Overall, there was a notable decline in local fixed line calls over the period, while both national and international fixed line calls and mobile calls exhibited an upward trajectory. This indicates a significant shift in communication preferences among Australians.

In detail, local fixed line calls decreased from 73 billion minutes in 2001 to 73 billion minutes in 2008, with a peak of 90 billion minutes in 2005 before experiencing a downward trend. Conversely, national and international fixed line calls rose steadily from 38 billion minutes in 2001 to 58 billion minutes by 2008, demonstrating a consistent increase each year. Mobile calls showed the most dramatic growth, escalating from a mere 3 billion minutes in 2001 to 48 billion minutes in 2008, with an especially sharp rise noted in the latter years of the period.

This data reflects a clear transition in communication habits, with a diminishing reliance on local fixed lines and a marked increase in the use of mobile technology.
