The chart illustrates the distribution of telephone calls in Australia across three categories—local fixed line calls, national and international fixed line calls, and mobile calls—over the period from 2001 to 2008.

Overall, there is a notable decline in local fixed line calls, while both national and international fixed line calls and mobile calls show an increasing trend. Local fixed line calls decreased from 73 billion minutes in 2001 to 73 billion minutes again in 2008, although there were fluctuations in between. In contrast, national and international fixed line calls rose steadily from 38 billion minutes in 2001 to 58 billion minutes by 2008, reflecting a significant increase in communication over longer distances.

Mobile calls experienced the most dramatic growth, starting at just 3 billion minutes in 2001 and reaching 48 billion minutes in 2008. This rise indicates a shift in consumer preference towards mobile communication over traditional fixed line options. Notably, the year 2007 marked a peak for mobile calls at 38 billion minutes, highlighting a surge in mobile usage just before a slight increase in fixed line calls the following year. The upward trajectory of national and international fixed line calls suggests a growing tendency for Australians to engage in long-distance communication.
