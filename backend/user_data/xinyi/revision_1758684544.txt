The chart illustrates the volume of telephone calls in Australia across three categories—local fixed line calls, national and international fixed line calls, and mobile calls—over an eight-year period from 2001 to 2008.

Overall, while local fixed line calls and national and international fixed line calls exhibited a decline towards the end of the period, mobile calls experienced a significant increase, highlighting a shift in communication preferences among Australians.

In detail, local fixed line calls started at 73 billion minutes in 2001, peaking at 90 billion minutes in 2005 before decreasing to 73 billion minutes by 2008. Similarly, national and international fixed line calls rose steadily from 38 billion minutes in 2001 to 58 billion minutes in 2008, although the growth was less pronounced in the latter years. In contrast, mobile calls showed a remarkable upward trend, rising from just 3 billion minutes in 2001 to 48 billion minutes in 2008. This sharp increase indicates a growing reliance on mobile technology for communication over the observed period. 

The data clearly reflects a transition from traditional fixed line calls to mobile communication, suggesting a significant change in consumer behavior in Australia during these years.
