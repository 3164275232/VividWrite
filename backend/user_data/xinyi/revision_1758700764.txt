The data illustrates the trends in telephone calls in Australia across three categories—local fixed line calls, national and international fixed line calls, and mobile calls—from 2001 to 2008. Overall, there is a noticeable shift in communication preferences, particularly with the rise of mobile calls, while local fixed line calls exhibit a decline.

In terms of results, local fixed line calls started at 73 billion minutes in 2001, peaked at 90 billion minutes in 2005, and ultimately fell to 73 billion minutes by 2008. National and international fixed line calls experienced a steady increase, rising from 38 billion minutes in 2001 to 58 billion minutes in 2008. Mobile calls showed significant growth, beginning at just 3 billion minutes in 2001 and escalating to 48 billion minutes by 2008, with a particularly sharp rise noted in the latter years.

The reference and explanation of these trends indicate that while local fixed line calls have remained stable at the beginning and end of the period, their overall trend reflects a decline in usage. In contrast, the growth in national and international fixed line calls and the substantial increase in mobile calls suggest a shift towards more flexible communication methods. The increasing reliance on mobile technology likely reflects broader societal changes, including the growing accessibility of mobile devices and a preference for more versatile communication options. This indicates a significant transformation in how Australians engaged in telecommunication over the seven-year period.
